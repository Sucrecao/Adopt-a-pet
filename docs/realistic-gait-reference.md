# Natural gait references for the plush walk

These primary studies guide the motion relationships. They do not validate a generated kitten/puppy model, and adult animal measurements are not copied as this character's hidden-joint dimensions. Geometry and timing values in the candidate are authored animation parameters and must pass actual-frame review.

- [Bishop, Pai and Schmitt,2008: Whole Body Mechanics of Stealthy Walking in Cats](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2583958/). Six cats were filmed and force-measured during overground walking. Posture and same-side footfall timing varied; more flexed/crouched strides tended toward more evenly spaced contacts. Dogs discussed in the paper commonly use lower same-side phase lag. This supports different cat/dog timing rather than a universal quarter-cycle offset. The paper includes crouched and extended real-cat walking clips. It does not justify exaggerated decorative torso bouncing; center-of-mass behavior depends on posture and phase.
- [English,1978: Functional analysis of the shoulder girdle of cats during locomotion](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/642016/). Slow-motion film and muscle recordings show scapular motion relative to the trunk through stance/swing. A fully frozen shoulder with distal-only skin translation omits an important part of forelimb reach.
- [1996: Kinematic analysis of the cat shoulder girdle during treadmill locomotion](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8714697/). X-ray observations describe scapular translation/rotation and load yielding following contact. Use a moving authored shoulder root; do not pretend a single visible fur bend is the anatomical shoulder joint.
- [1996: Kinematic analysis of cat hindlimb stepping](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8747190/). Limb segment timing shapes whole-limb length/orientation during stance and swing. Fixed bone lengths can still produce a shorter effective limb through articulation; this is distinct from shrinking the photographic skin or bone itself.
- [2003: Two-dimensional link-segment model of the forelimb of dogs at a walk](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12755302/). A four-segment forelimb model combines normal-dog motion and force-platform data through stance. Separate shoulder/elbow/carpus/distal-paw roles; one translating paw and fixed fractional bend are not equivalent.
- [2022: Development of a detailed canine gait analysis method for evaluating harnesses](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0264299). The method tracks body/spinal and all-limb joint angles, plus paw trajectories. Heads move relative to the trunk and are not a dependable proxy for body travel; stabilizing the face does not require freezing the entire shoulder/back.

## Authored implementation implications

The first candidate uses a lateral sequence with cat same-side lag around.25 and dog around.15; these are candidate choices for a calm plush walk, not universal biological constants. Support lasts longer than swing. Stride centers lie under the authored shoulder/hip projections, while the neutral standing footprint can lie behind them. Preserve each segment length, permit correct elbow/stifle/hock articulation, and reject unreachable geometry.

The world-space swing uses a quintic minimum-jerk interpolation between consecutive contacts. Subtract root travel to obtain the local paw path. This directly repairs the measured velocity discontinuity in0.4.4. Fourth-power-sine lift is a convenient initial C2 envelope; its exact height/shape requires visual calibration. The papers above do not claim that either formula is the measured trajectory of every cat/dog.

Load, shoulder/pelvis movement, distal-paw pivot and depth occlusion must appear in the rendered character. Evaluate both the individual pose and the consecutive-frame motion. Foot-lock math alone is insufficient, as the independently rejected0.4.4 frames show.
